## IELTS 8.0

### 5 words / collocations
1. **bolster** (v) – củng cố, tăng cường. *Tourism can bolster a struggling local economy.* Lỗi: dùng cho thứ xấu. Thay "strengthen".
2. **detrimental to** – có hại cho. *Excessive screen time is detrimental to children's concentration.* Lỗi: "detrimental for/of". Thay "bad for".
3. **tangible benefits** – lợi ích hữu hình, thấy rõ. *Flexible hours bring tangible benefits to working parents.* Lỗi: "tangible" không đi với cảm xúc trừu tượng. Thay "clear benefits".
4. **stem from** (phr. v) – bắt nguồn từ. *Many health problems stem from poor diet.* Lỗi: "stem from to". Thay "be caused by".
5. **a pressing issue** – vấn đề cấp bách. *Housing costs are a pressing issue for young people.* Lỗi: "pressure issue". Thay "an important problem".

### Grammar for Writing: Cleft sentence (câu nhấn mạnh)
- Form: **It is/was + [phần cần nhấn] + that/who + ...**
- Example: *It is long-term investment, not short-term profit, that bolsters an economy.*
- Task 2: dùng 1 lần ở câu luận điểm hoặc để đối chiếu hai ý. Đừng lạm dụng (tối đa 1–2 lần).

### Model answer for yesterday's prompt (Task 2, Opinion: public transport vs new roads)
"Some argue that public money should go to public transport rather than new roads. I largely agree, although roads still have a role.

Firstly, congestion stems from too many private cars, and new roads rarely solve this: they tend to attract more traffic. By contrast, a reliable metro or bus network carries far more people per square metre. It is investment in such systems that delivers tangible benefits, namely shorter commutes and cleaner air. Were governments to prioritise rail, fewer residents would feel obliged to drive.

Secondly, public transport promotes fairness. Low-income families, the elderly and students often cannot afford cars, so affordable buses give them access to jobs and healthcare. Not only does this widen opportunity, but it also bolsters the local economy.

Admittedly, rural areas with scattered populations may still need well-maintained roads, and freight depends on them. However, this need is limited and should not dominate the budget.

In conclusion, the balance of evidence favours spending on public transport, with roads funded selectively."

**Why it scores (3 dòng):**
1. Task Response: nêu quan điểm rõ ngay từ đầu, có nhượng bộ (Admittedly) rồi kết luận nhất quán.
2. Lexical resource: collocation chính xác (stem from, tangible benefits, bolster), tránh lặp từ.
3. Grammar range: đảo ngữ điều kiện, Not only..., cleft sentence, kết hợp câu phức tự nhiên.

## Everyday English

### Scenario: Airport check-in
- Agent: Good morning. Where are you flying to today?
- You: Da Nang, please. Here's my passport.
- Agent: Thanks. Are you checking any bags?
- You: Just this one. It's a bit heavy, I'm afraid. Is it over the limit?
- Agent: Let me see... it's fine, 19 kilos. Would you like a window or an aisle seat?
- You: A window seat, if possible.
- Agent: Done. Boarding starts at 9:40 at Gate 12.
- You: Sorry, which gate again?
- Agent: Gate 12. Just follow the signs after security.
- You: Great, thanks a lot!

### Say it like a native
| Textbook | More natural |
|---|---|
| Where is the gate? | How do I get to Gate 12? |
| Can you repeat? | Sorry, could you say that again? |
| My bag is too heavy. | I think I've overpacked. |
| I want a seat near the window. | Could I have a window seat, if possible? |
| My flight is delayed. | My flight's been pushed back. |

### 2 idioms / phrasal verbs
- **sort out** – giải quyết, xử lý. *Could you help me sort out a booking error?*
- **give someone a heads-up** – báo trước cho ai. *Thanks for the heads-up about the delay.*

### Shadowing
1. "Could I have a window seat?" – nối "have a" → /hæ-və/; nhấn **window**.
2. "Which gate again?" – nối "gate again" → /geɪ-tə-gen/; nhấn **gate**.
3. "Thanks for the heads-up." – "for the" yếu /fə-ðə/; nhấn **thanks**, **heads**.
